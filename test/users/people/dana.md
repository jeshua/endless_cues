# Dana, stage manager for a dance company

## Who

Stage manager for a contemporary dance company of seven that tours small venues: a different room every week, sometimes with a house technician, often without. Calls the show and runs the sound herself from a laptop. Not a sound person. Has operated QLab shows that other people built, pressing GO and occasionally nudging a level, and has built two simple ones herself by copying what she had seen. Knows what a cue, a fade and an auto-follow are; has never opened most of QLab's inspector tabs.

Careful, methodical, a little anxious about sound because when it goes wrong it is her fault in front of an audience. Reads what is on the screen. Will read a help page if there is one.

## The show

An evening of five pieces to recorded music, about an hour, with house music before and bows after. Nothing is built: the choreographer sent a folder of tracks and a page of notes.

## Why she is here

The company's laptop is a Windows machine. She has been borrowing a Mac for QLab and wants to stop.

## On the desk

- `tour music/`: seven tracks
- `running order.txt`: the choreographer's notes

## Tasks

1. **build** Start a new show and get the seven tracks into it in running order, each named and numbered so you could call it.
2. **house** Make the house music loop until you take it out, and make a cue that fades it out over 8 seconds and stops it.
3. **trim** Make Undertow start 12 seconds into the file, and end at 0:40 with a 5-second fade out.
4. **follow** Make Driftwood go into Lantern with no GO from you, with 3 seconds of silence between them.
5. **level** Take Salt down 4 dB.
6. **save** Save the show to a folder on the desk. Close the app, open it again, and check the show is as you left it.
7. **rehearse** A dancer asks to take Salt from 0:30. Do it. Then again.
8. **perform** Lock the show so nothing can be changed by accident, and run the whole evening from house music to bows, checking after each GO that what should be sounding is. (The tracks are short stand-ins. You need not sit through each: once you know it is right, move on in whatever way the app offers.)
9. **help** Find out, from the app alone, what the keys are for GO, stop everything, and going back one cue.

## What she judges it on

- Could she build this show the afternoon before a performance with nobody to ask?
- Does the screen tell her what will happen when she presses GO?
- Is there anything she could press by accident during a show that would hurt?
- Are the words on the screen ones she knows?
